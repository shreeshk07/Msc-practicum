# Participant Information Sheet

**Study title:** Automated Phishing Email Triage, A Comparison of Rule-Based Automation Against Manual Analyst Triage

You are being invited to take part in a research study. Before you decide, please read this sheet so that you understand why the research is being done and what taking part will involve. Take as much time as you need, and ask the researcher about anything that is not clear.

## What the study is about

The study looks at how quickly and how accurately people can triage phishing emails by hand, and compares that with an automated tool that uses a fixed set of rules to reach the same kind of decision. The research question is whether a rule-based automated triage tool can reduce the average time to triage an email compared with manual analyst triage, while keeping decision accuracy at a comparable level in a security operations workflow. As part of this, the study also gathers your views on the explanations that the automated tool produces.

## Why you have been invited

You have been invited because you are either a working security operations analyst or a student with relevant background. The study groups participants into two strata, SOC analyst and student, so that the two groups can be compared. You do not need to prepare anything in advance.

## Do you have to take part

No. Taking part is completely voluntary. If you decide to take part you will be asked to sign a consent form, and you are still free to stop at any time. Deciding not to take part, or stopping partway through, will not affect you in any way.

## What taking part involves

You will attend one session that lasts about two hours. In the session you will review twenty emails, one at a time, on a simple web form. For each email you will decide whether it is phishing or benign, give a short reason for your decision, and record how confident you are. The form records how long you spend on each email. After the twentieth email you will answer a short questionnaire of nine statements about the automated tool and its explanations, with the option to add a comment in your own words. You will be offered a break every thirty minutes, and you may pause at any time.

## Your right to withdraw

You can stop at any point during the session without giving a reason. You can also ask the researcher to remove your data up to one week after your session. After that point the results are combined and anonymised for analysis, so it is no longer possible to find and remove your individual contribution.

## What data is collected

The form records only the following: your pseudonym, your stratum, your verdict for each email, the time you take on each email, your confidence level, and your answers to the nine questionnaire items. It also keeps the short free text reason you give for each decision, the optional comment at the end, and a count of how many checklist steps you ticked. No name, email address, IP address, or demographic data is stored in the results. Please do not type anything that identifies you into the free text boxes.

## How your data is stored and kept anonymous

You are identified in the results only by a pseudonym, for example P1 through P10. The only record that links your pseudonym to your name is held in a separate encrypted KeePassXC file. The results files and that mapping file are stored on an encrypted laptop. The mapping file is deleted when the dissertation is submitted, after which the results cannot be traced back to you.

## How long the data is kept

The results are kept for thirty days after the final submission of the dissertation and are then deleted.

## Lawful basis for processing your data

The lawful basis for processing your data is your consent, under Article 6(1)(a) of the General Data Protection Regulation. You may withdraw that consent as described in the section on your right to withdraw.

## Risks and discomfort

The risk to you is minimal. The only likely discomfort is screen fatigue from reading emails for a period of time, which is why a break is offered every thirty minutes and you may rest whenever you wish. There is no deception of any kind in this study. All of the emails come from public research datasets and contain no live or active malicious content. The emails are shown to you as plain text, links are not clickable, and no attachment is ever opened.

## Are there any benefits

There is no direct personal benefit to you. By taking part you help build evidence about whether rule-based automation can support phishing triage in a security operations team, which is the purpose of the research.

## What happens to the results

The combined, anonymised results are used in the researcher's MSc dissertation and may appear in any output that comes from it. No individual participant is identifiable in anything that is written up or shared.
